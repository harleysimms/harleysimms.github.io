ABOUT ME

I’ve spent my career working inside the systems schools depend on— and increasingly, building better ones.

My work sits at the intersection of education, technology, systems design, leadership, and practical problem solving.

MY STORY

Education first. Technology with purpose.

I began my career in the classroom, where I learned firsthand how technology, data, assessment, and administrative systems affect the daily work of teachers and students.

Over time, I found myself increasingly drawn to the problems behind those systems: repetitive workflows, disconnected information, difficult-to-use tools, and processes that could be made more efficient, reliable, and understandable.

That curiosity has led me into educational technology, large-scale assessment operations, Python automation, API integrations, web application development, database-driven systems, staff training, and organizational leadership.

What connects all of that work is a simple goal: understand the problem first, then use the right combination of technology, process improvement, communication, and training to solve it.

HOW I WORK

Three perspectives shape the way I approach problems.

01

I understand classroom realities, instructional needs, student support, assessment demands, and the practical impact technology decisions have on teachers and students.

WHAT DRIVES MY WORK

Better systems should make people’s work easier.

Solve the real problem

Technology should not be introduced simply because it is available. I start by understanding the actual problem, the people affected by it, and the outcome that needs to improve.

Reduce unnecessary complexity

I look for opportunities to eliminate redundant steps, connect disconnected information, automate repetitive work, and make systems easier to navigate.

Build with the user in mind

A technically impressive solution is not useful if the people who need it cannot understand or confidently use it.

Make improvement sustainable

Good systems should be documented, understandable, maintainable, and designed to continue working after the initial implementation.

MY PHILOSOPHY

Technology should remove barriers, not create new ones.

The best technology is not necessarily the newest or most complicated. It is the technology that solves a meaningful problem, saves people time, improves access to information, and supports better work.
